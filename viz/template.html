Every tissue has a chemical fingerprint

Real REIMS spectra from 72 patients, and a classifier that never saw the patients you are about to explore. Everything on this page is real data and real model output.

1 · The ion plume

An electrosurgical knife vaporises tissue. The smoke carries its lipids as ions into a mass spectrometer, which sorts them by mass-to-charge (m/z). Each dot below is a share of the real averaged signal of that tissue, flying to its place on the m/z axis.

Fat-storage lipids dominate the right (800–1000), membrane lipids the middle (600–800), free fatty acids the left (200–400). Class averages of the real spectra.

2 · The tissue galaxy

All 689 spectra projected onto the two directions that best separate the tissues (learned from training patients only). Rings mark spectra from the 18 patients held out from training. A pulsing amber ring is a spectrum the model got wrong. Hover or click a ringed point. The overlap in the middle is why 91%, not 100%.

3 · The live scalpel

Drag the scalpel (mouse, finger or Apple Pencil) across the tissue. Each pass pulls a real spectrum from the held-out patients for that region. The model sees only the numbers, never the region. It sometimes hesitates and sometimes errs, and the tally keeps count.
